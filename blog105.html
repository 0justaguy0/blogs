1. Introduction: The Paradox of "Nothing Ever Happened"

In the high-altitude philosophy of Advaita Vedanta, there exists a position so radical it appears to dismantle the very foundations of human experience. This is the doctrine of Ajata—literally "not created" or "not caused." While our senses report a world of constant birth and decay, the Ajata doctrine posits that the universe was never actually brought into existence.

Bhagavan Ramana Maharshi, the Sage of Arunachala, consistently maintained that this was the "ultimate truth." He frequently cited Gaudapada’s Mandukya Upanishad Karika, which he rendered into Tamil with uncompromising clarity:

"There is no creation, no destruction, no bondage, no longing to be freed from bondage, no striving to be free, nor anyone who has attained freedom. Know that this is the ultimate truth."

To the modern mind, this sounds like a frequency mismatch between philosophical idealism and empirical reality. How do we reconcile this "nothingness" with the precise, mathematical clockwork of the cosmos? This exploration seeks to bridge the absolute silence of Ajata with the technical mechanics of Jyotish (Vedic Astrology)—the "life side" of astronomy that maps the vibrations of our perceived existence.

—

2. The Absolute Truth of Ajata: Why Ramana Maharshi Taught Non-Creation

For the realized Jnani, Ajata is not a theory; it is the direct experience of the Self. However, recognizing that the human mind is addicted to causality, Ramana Maharshi and Papaji categorized creation theories into a hierarchy based on the seeker's capacity to digest the truth:

  1. Ajata Vada: The highest truth; the position that nothing exists except the one reality (Brahman).
  2. Drishti-Srishti Vada: The "simultaneous creation" view; the argument that the seen depends entirely on the seer, much like a dream.
  3. Srishti-Drishti Vada: The "beginner" view; the causal sequence where God creates the world and its elements over time.

To understand why Ajata is the "true experience," a scholar must distinguish between "existence" and "reality." In the Vedic context, Sat (Reality) is defined specifically as that which is permanent, unchanging, and self-luminous. Contrast this with the Loka (world), which literally means "that which is seen." Because the perceived world is fleeting and dependent on a knower (Pramata), it fails the test of Sat.

As Papaji noted, the very act of creation would imply an "unsatisfied desire" or incompleteness in the Absolute. Since the Self is perfect, creation never truly occurred; it is merely a "self-forgetfulness" where infinite consciousness views itself as the object of perception.

3. The Physics of the Illusion: Jyotish as the "Life Side" of Astronomy

If Ajata is the truth of the substratum, Jyotish is the science of the Pramata’s (the knower’s) environment. Dr. Y.V. Subba Rao defines Jyotish as the study of the effect of sunlight on Earth—the "life side" of astronomy. It does not concern itself with the absolute, but with the "vibrations" of the dream.

This science employs "Selective Adaptation," including or excluding celestial bodies based on their energetic impact on the 120-year human lifespan:

  • Exclusions:
  • The Earth: Excluded because it is the moving observatory from which we view the spectrum.
  • Outer Planets (Uranus, Neptune, Pluto): While known to ancient sages (referenced by Veda Vyasa as Shveta, Shyama, and Teevra), they are traditionally omitted. Not only is their reflected sunlight negligible, but their orbital periods exceed a human lifespan, making them impractical for the predictive rules of the 120-year Vimsottari system.
  • Inclusions:
  • The Sun: The star signifies the soul (Atmakaraka) and the primary source of electromagnetic energy.
  • The Moon: Regulates "psychological time" and reflects solar energy.
  • Lunar Nodes (Rahu and Ketu): These "shadowy planets" are included because they modulate the sunlight incident on the Moon, creating the fluctuations of the two fortnights.

5. Matter, Energy, and the Self: A Scientific Synthesis

The bridge between physics and Vedanta is found in the nature of vibration. Albert Einstein famously stated: "What we have called matter is really energy, whose vibration has been lowered as to be perceivable to the senses." This is the scientific equivalent of the Vedantic concept of Maya—a "frequency mismatch" where the mind perceives the slow vibration of form rather than the high-frequency light of the Self.

The illusion of causality—the belief that the planets "cause" our fate—is corrected by the Kaka-Akshi-Nyaya (The Crow and the Coconut) logic from the Yoga Vasistha. If a crow lands on a tree and a coconut falls at that exact moment, the observer assumes the crow caused the fall. In reality, it was an "accidental coincidence." Similarly, the "Law of Cause and Effect" is merely a mental habit born from the accidental coincidence of planetary vibrations and human tendencies. In the Absolute, the "unborn" cannot become "born."

6. Actionable Insights: Abiding in the Truth

While Jyotish maps the weather of our lives, the goal of the seeker is to find the one who is beyond the weather. Based on the "Instruction into Truth" and the "Siva Puja" teachings, we find three core pillars for practice:

  1. Discrimination (Viveka): Constantly differentiate between Sat (the permanent, self-luminous Self) and Asat (the fleeting world). Remember: the world is "unreal" when perceived apart from the Self, but "real" when known as the Self.
  2. Effortlessness: As the Tao Te Ching and Maharshi suggest, "The Kingdom is a spirit-like thing" that cannot be gained through "active doing." Effort is only the breaking of a habit—the habit of calling "things" anything other than God.
  3. The Heartward View: Turning the mind inward reveals a profound unity. The source text notes: "That which is declared to be peace in the Heartward view is itself power in the outward view." Peace and power are one when the mind is at rest. The authentic Siva Puja is not an external ritual, but this state of profound, thought-free peace.

—

7. Conclusion: Returning to the Source

The light of the stars and the light of the Self are not two. Jyotish provides the map of the "appearance," but Ajata provides the truth of the "essence." We study the movement of the planets only to realize that the one who observes them is as still as a rock, motionless and unassailable.

The ultimate spiritual transformation is represented by Jnana Vibhuti—the sacred ash. Just as the cow dung of the ego is calcined in the fire of Self-knowledge, only the ash of "Absolute Being" remains. This fire transforms the three worlds into a single mass of "red light," symbolized by the sacred kumkum. In this silence, we finally understand: while the stars dance, the Self remains unborn. Nothing ever happened.
